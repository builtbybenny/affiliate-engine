# Stack & Save copy quality bar

Every generated post and product-row fallback follows these principles. The aim is clear, particular writing that earns attention through relevance and usefulness, not fake certainty.

## Evidence and trust
- The active product row is the factual source of truth: product name, listed benefits, specs, price and trial terms. Repeat those accurately; do not imply more than they say.
- Research keywords and buyer questions can help with reader vocabulary. They are not evidence. Cached facts without a verifiable source URL, date, relevance and current terms are not publishable claims.
- Do not invent numbers, outcomes, time savings, customer stories, adoption, popularity, urgency, personal use or tests. A vivid scene describes the reader's recognizable situation; it must not pretend a result happened.
- If proof is missing, say less. Never make a weak claim sound stronger by changing adjectives or adding plausible-sounding detail.

## The opening and the argument
- Lead with one concrete reader situation, decision or product detail. Name the product naturally when it helps the reader understand what is being discussed; never hide the subject just to force curiosity.
- The opening makes a promise the post immediately repays. Avoid broad claims that would fit any SaaS product, vague suspense, false questions, competitor-swappable hooks and unsupported numbers.
- Move through one useful point: what the reader is deciding or doing, what the product row actually confirms, why that detail matters, and one clear next step.
- Reels: the first frame is understandable without sound; pay off the opening quickly. Carousels: make the cover's promise truthful and give each interior slide one distinct reason to continue.
- Keep copy as short as the idea permits. No arbitrary word quota, repeated keywords, padding or lists added just to fill slides.

## Human voice, not a “humanizer” pass
- Write like a careful person who knows the product facts: specific nouns and verbs, plain language, varied sentence length, and no forced catchphrases.
- Rewrite from the evidence and audience situation. Do not run synonym replacement or add fake imperfections to disguise generated text.
- Avoid contrast reveals (“not X, but Y”), negation lists, trailing benefit piles, rhetorical questions that answer themselves, canned openers, repetitive short-line “broetry,” emoji bullets, and em dashes in short social copy.
- Remove “game-changing,” “revolutionary,” “next-level,” “unlock the power,” “secret,” “hidden,” “everyone is switching,” “most requested,” and any other claim of exclusivity, urgency, popularity or novelty without evidence.
- Never use engagement bait such as “Thoughts?”, “Agree?”, “tag a friend,” or an empty request to save/share. Ask a question only when the reader's answer would add real value; otherwise land the point.

## CTA, disclosure, platform constraints
- One direct action only: check the current offer via the link in bio. Do not put affiliate URLs in the caption or comments.
- Preserve the exact existing affiliate disclosure: “Affiliate link — we may earn a small commission at no extra cost to you.”
- Preserve Instagram's five-hashtag maximum and the existing hashtag rotation. Hashtags do not replace useful copy.
- Never imply plan terms are permanent. Refer to the catalog's listed terms and invite the reader to check the live offer.

## Release checklist
Before copy reaches rendering, confirm:
1. Hook is short, readable, relevant and paid off by the post.
2. Every factual number or specific product claim is supported by the current product row; no cached unsupported research claim is used.
3. No fabricated first-person account, review, popularity, result, urgency or cliché remains.
4. Slides have distinct lines and the caption begins with the chosen hook.
5. There is one honest link-in-bio CTA, the affiliate disclosure is present, and caption length/hashtag rules are respected.
6. The same criteria work from the data row for a product added in the future.
