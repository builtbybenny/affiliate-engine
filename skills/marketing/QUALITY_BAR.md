# QUALITY BAR — every caption, hook and slide obeys every line

## Hard bans (a single use is a rewrite)
- Contrast reveals: "Not X. Y." / "It's not X, it's Y." / "Not just X, but Y." /
  "X? Think again." State Y directly, with its concrete reason attached.
- Negation lists: "No X, no Y, no Z" / "Without X, without Y" / "Zero X, zero
  Y". Say what DOES happen, in the order it happens. One plain "No card
  required" beside the CTA is allowed; a list of absences is not.
- Trailing pile-ons: a complete claim followed by ", no exports, no
  spreadsheets..." or ", ensuring nothing slips". End the sentence at the
  claim; the next benefit gets its own line.
- Bait vocabulary: game-changing, revolutionary, disruptive, next-level,
  10x, secret, hidden, "worth $X" without a comparable, "limited time"
  without a real limit, "100% guaranteed" without conditions.
- Hedged openers and vague numbers: "can help", "may improve", "up to",
  "industry-leading". Use the researched number or say nothing.

## Carousel decks (carousel-frameworks.md — pick ONE, obey it end to end)
- Exactly one architecture per deck: Value-Stack, Problem-Proof, Hack List,
  Rant Callout, or Demo Walkthrough. A carousel is not a blog post chopped
  into squares; each slide has two jobs — deliver one idea, and make the
  next swipe irresistible.
- Slide 1 is the thumbnail: it competes in the feed alone, so it must stop
  a scroll by itself (specific count/deliverable for Value-Stack, a
  result-with-number claim for Problem-Proof).
- One idea per slide, and a slide line NEVER repeats within a deck —
  shipping the same line twice (beehiiv 10-14, slides 1+2) is the clearest
  possible "swipe to the same slide" kill signal.
- Interior slides may name the tool once it is introduced; every line still
  under 12 words.
- No slide may exist to fill space: a deck is honest to its exact count
  ("6 slides" = 6 slides of substance).
- The close converts the swipe-through: free trial, link in bio — never a
  raw URL.

## Selling, not informing (our addition — 2026-10-25 content rebuild)
- One journey gap per post: the gap they feel -> this tool closes it (one
  checkable fact) -> their week after -> ONE ask. A post that teaches three
  things and never asks for the click has failed.
- Banned claims, no exceptions: "most requested", "you keep asking", "back
  again / here it is again", "X people grabbed this", "hundreds/thousands of
  users", "everyone is switching", "trending", and any invented popularity,
  sales or usage number. We have no data for any of them.
- No feature tours: never open or close with a list of what the tool does
  ("it has funnels, email, courses..."). The reader buys the after-state.
- The hook is line 1 and scene 1 — exactly once. It never returns as a later
  caption line, a slide, or an on-screen reminder.
- The caption ends on a decision: one free-trial line, "link in bio", no menu
  of options, no "save this for later" as the closing move.
- Never restate the product's benefit lines; translate each one into the
  viewer's moment, phrased differently from the last post.

## Composition rules
- Clarity beats cleverness. A line that needs a re-read is a rewrite.
- Specificity beats vagueness: "Cut weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15
  minutes", never "save time on reporting".
- Benefits before features: feature = what it does; benefit = what that
  means for the viewer's day.
- Customer language over company language: use the words from the researched
  objections, not SaaS jargon.
- One idea per line. Each bullet carries exactly one concrete benefit,
  ideally with its researched number.
- Hook shapes to draft from (the scoring pass still picks the winner):
  curiosity — "I was wrong about [belief]", "[Outcome] usually comes down to
  [surprising cause]"; story — "Last week, [unexpected thing]", "3 years ago
  I [past]. Today, [now]"; value — lead with the concrete number saved.
- The first line owns everything: it must stop a scroll inside one second
  without naming a tool.
